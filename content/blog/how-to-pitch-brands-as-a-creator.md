Waiting for brands to find you is the slowest way to grow as a creator. The creators landing consistent deals are almost always the ones pitching first. The good news: most creators pitch badly, so a clear, confident pitch stands out immediately. Here is how to do it.

## Before you pitch: have your kit ready

Never send a cold pitch without a media kit link. The brand's first question is "who is this and are they worth my time," and your pitch should answer it in one click. If you have not built one yet, start with [how to make a media kit](/blog/how-to-make-a-media-kit).

A link beats an attachment here for a practical reason: attachments get caught in spam filters and feel heavy in a cold email. A single line like "here are my live stats" with a link is frictionless.

## Step 1: Pick the right brands

Do not pitch everyone. Pitch brands where the fit is obvious:

- Brands you **already use and mention** (the easiest yes)
- Brands whose **customer is your audience**, backed by your demographics
- Brands **already working with creators your size** (they have a budget and a process)

A shortlist of 20 well-matched brands beats a blast to 200.

## Step 2: Find the right person

Do not send it to a generic info@ address. Look for:

- A marketing, partnerships, or influencer manager on LinkedIn
- A "collabs" or "creators" email on their site
- The person who replied to other creators in their comments

One real human contact is worth more than ten generic inboxes.

## Step 3: Write the pitch

Keep it short. A busy marketer skims. Lead with the fit, prove it with one stat, make the ask, and link your kit.

**Cold pitch template:**

```
Subject: [Your niche] creator + [Brand]

Hi [name],

I am [your name], a [niche] creator with [X followers] and a
[engagement rate] engagement rate. My audience is largely
[key demographic], which lines up closely with [Brand]'s customer.

I already [use/love] [specific product], and I would love to
create content featuring it. Here is my live media kit with
current stats and audience data: [link].

Would you be open to a quick chat about a collaboration?

Thanks,
[name]
```

**Warmer version, if you already engage with them:**

```
Subject: Loved [recent campaign] + a collab idea

Hi [name],

Your [recent campaign/product] is exactly the kind of thing
my audience responds to. I am a [niche] creator with a
[demographic] following that overlaps closely with yours.

I have an idea for [specific content format] featuring
[product]. Full stats here: [link].

Open to exploring it?

[name]
```

## Step 4: Follow up once

If you do not hear back in a week, send one short follow-up:

```
Hi [name], just floating this back up in case it got buried.
Still keen to collaborate whenever the timing works. Thanks!
```

One follow-up is professional. Three is annoying. Send one, then move on.

## What actually gets replies

Three things separate pitches that get answered from pitches that get deleted:

1. **Specific fit.** "Your customer is my audience, here is the data" beats "I love your brand."
2. **Proof they can trust.** A live, verifiable kit removes the doubt that kills cold pitches. If the brand clicks your profile and the numbers match your kit exactly, you look credible instead of inflated.
3. **A clear, small ask.** "Open to a quick chat?" is easy to say yes to. "Please sponsor me" is not.
