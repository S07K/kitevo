When creators search for media kit examples, they are usually looking for one thing: a template they can copy without guessing. The problem with most examples online is that they show a pretty layout but hide the thinking behind it. A media kit is not a design exercise. It is a decision tool for a brand. This guide breaks down what a strong one contains, and how it shifts depending on the kind of creator you are.

If you have not yet, start with our guide on [how to make a media kit](/blog/how-to-make-a-media-kit) for the fundamentals. This post is about seeing them applied.

## The universal structure

Almost every effective media kit, regardless of niche or size, follows the same order:

1. **Who you are** — one line, your niche
2. **The numbers** — followers, engagement, reach, growth
3. **Who your audience is** — age, gender, location
4. **Proof** — best posts and past brand work
5. **How to work with you** — contact and rates

What changes between creators is not the structure. It is which parts you lean on.

## Example 1: The niche micro-creator

Say you are a 12,000-follower creator in a tight niche, like sustainable skincare. Your follower count is small, so you do not lead with it. You lead with **engagement and audience fit.**

A strong kit here emphasizes:

- **Engagement rate** front and center (a 7 percent rate on 12k is far more impressive than 1 percent on 200k)
- **Audience demographics** that scream "your customer" — for skincare, that might be 78 percent women aged 25 to 34
- Two or three posts that drove real comments and saves

The pitch this kit makes: "I am small, but every follower is exactly who you are trying to reach, and they actually listen to me."

## Example 2: The travel creator

A travel creator's strength is visual proof and reach across formats. Their kit leans on:

- **Total audience across platforms** (Instagram plus YouTube plus maybe TikTok), because travel brands care about combined reach
- A **top-locations breakdown** — a brand running a campaign in Southeast Asia wants to see your audience is there
- **Follower growth**, because travel accounts often grow in visible waves that signal momentum
- A grid of their most striking recent posts

The pitch: "Wide, engaged reach with an audience concentrated exactly where your campaign runs."

## Example 3: The YouTuber

A YouTuber's numbers look different, so their kit does too. It leads on:

- **Subscribers and total views**, plus **average views per video** (a truer measure than subscriber count)
- **Watch-time relevant proof** — link a couple of videos that performed well
- Any long-term brand integrations, since those signal you can carry a sponsor across a full video

The pitch: "Deep, sustained attention, not a fleeting scroll."

## The section-by-section template

Copy this and fill it in:

- **Intro line:** "[Niche] creator sharing [what you make] for [who]."
- **Core stats:** Followers / Subscribers · Engagement rate · Avg reach or views · 30-day growth
- **Audience:** Age split · Gender split · Top 3 locations
- **Recent work:** 3 to 6 of your best posts with their performance
- **Brands:** Logos or names of past partners (skip if none)
- **Contact and rates:** Email or DM handle · a starting rate or "rates on request"

That is a complete kit. Everything else is decoration.

## The thing every good example has in common

The best media kits share one quiet quality: **the numbers are current and believable.** A brand can spot a rounded-up or stale figure instantly, and it poisons the whole pitch. The strongest kits are the ones where a marketing manager can click through to your actual profile and see the numbers match.
