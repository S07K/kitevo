If you have ever pitched a brand and heard nothing back, your media kit might be the reason. Not because your audience is too small, but because the kit made a busy marketing manager work to find the one thing they care about: whether partnering with you is worth their budget.

A media kit is not a resume. It is a sales page for your influence. This guide walks through exactly what to put in one, what to leave out, and the single biggest upgrade most creators are missing.

## What a media kit actually is

A media kit is a short, shareable summary of who you are, who follows you, and what it looks like to work with you. Brands use it to answer three questions in under a minute:

1. **Is this creator's audience my customer?**
2. **Do the numbers back up the reach they claim?**
3. **How do I get in touch and what does it cost?**

If your kit answers those three questions fast and credibly, you are already ahead of most of the creators competing for the same deal.

## What to include (in order)

Order matters. Put the strongest, most scannable information first.

### 1. A one-line introduction

Who you are and what you make, in a sentence. "Travel and slow-living creator sharing budget trips across South Asia." A brand should know if you are a fit before they scroll.

### 2. Your core stats

The numbers a brand is actually deciding on:

- **Follower and subscriber counts** across the platforms you are pitching
- **Engagement rate** (average likes and comments relative to your reach), which often matters more than raw follower count
- **Average views or reach per post**
- **Follower growth** over the last month or two, if it is trending up

Show real numbers. A brand that later finds inflated stats will not just pass on this deal, they will remember.

### 3. Audience demographics

This is where mid-sized creators win. A brand does not just want reach, they want *their* customer. Include:

- **Age and gender breakdown**
- **Top locations** (countries or cities)
- Any niche detail that matters for your category (interests, buying behavior)

A creator with 15,000 highly relevant followers often beats one with 100,000 scattered ones. Demographics are how you prove it.

### 4. Recent work and best posts

Three to six of your strongest recent posts, ideally ones that performed well or fit the kind of brand you are pitching. Let them see your actual content, not just claims about it.

### 5. Past brand partnerships

If you have worked with brands before, list a few. Social proof lowers the perceived risk of being your next partner. If you are just starting, skip this rather than pad it.

### 6. Rates and contact

Make it effortless to say yes. A clear way to reach you, and if you are comfortable, a starting rate or a rate card. Ambiguity here is where a lot of deals quietly die.

## What to leave out

- **Long bios and life stories.** Save it. Brands are skimming.
- **Vanity metrics with no context.** "1 million impressions" means little without reach and engagement.
- **Every single post you have ever made.** Curate. Six great posts beat sixty average ones.
- **Fake or rounded-up numbers.** The fastest way to lose a repeat partner.

## The mistake almost everyone makes

Most creators build their media kit once, as a PDF, and email it out for the next year.

The problem: the day you export that PDF, it starts going out of date. Your follower count moves. Your engagement shifts. Your best post from three months ago gets replaced by a better one. By the time a brand opens it, the numbers no longer match your profile, and a marketing manager who cross-checks your account sees the gap immediately.

A static PDF also cannot show a brand your growth, and it cannot be verified. Anyone can type any number into a design tool. That is exactly the doubt you do not want in a decision-maker's mind while they are choosing between you and another creator.

## A simple checklist

Before you send your kit to a brand, make sure it has:

- A one-line intro that states your niche
- Real, current follower and engagement numbers
- Audience demographics (age, gender, top locations)
- Three to six of your best recent posts
- Any past brand work worth showing
- A clear way to reach you and a sense of your rates

Get those six things right, keep the numbers honest and current, and you will stand out from the pile of stale PDFs every brand is used to receiving.
